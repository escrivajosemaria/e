Anchoring the SDGs in Moral Leadership: East Africa's Development Through Mandela's Lens

This research examines how the Sustainable Development Goals are operationalized across East Africa, focusing on the gap between global development commitments and on-the-ground realities. Using a moral leadership framework inspired by Nelson Mandela, the study assesses progress and systemic constraints across poverty reduction, healthcare access, education, climate resilience, and governance. The project offers a critical evaluation of implementation mechanisms, monitoring practices, and institutional accountability, generating insights aimed at strengthening policy coherence and advancing the region's progress toward the 2030 Agenda.

Unraveling Factors Predisposing Young Adults Aged 18–25 to Mental Health Problems

This publication investigates the structural, social, and psychological factors contributing to mental health challenges among university-aged young adults. Drawing on empirical data, the study explores how academic pressure, social expectations, economic uncertainty, and limited mental health support systems intersect to elevate risks of stress, anxiety, and depression. Beyond diagnosis, the research calls for systemic reform in how institutions address youth mental well-being, positioning mental health as a public health and development priority rather than an individual failing.

Risk Factors of Malnutrition Among Children Aged 6–59 Months in Kiminini Sub-County, Trans Nzoia County, Kenya

This facility-based, cross-sectional study evaluates the socio-economic, bio-demographic, and feeding-related risk factors associated with malnutrition among children aged 6 to 59 months. Conducted at St. Martha Hospital with primary data collected from 319 caregivers, malnutrition risk was operationalized using low dietary diversity, defined as consuming fewer than 3 food groups, and 37.6% of children were classified as being at risk on this basis. Maternal education emerged as the statistically significant factor associated with malnutrition risk in bivariate analysis, while household income, breastfeeding practices, and distance to the facility were not statistically significant at the 5% level.

Research approach

Each stage of the process is structured for rigour, relevance, and real-world utility: evidence that can be applied to policy decisions, program design, and community-level action.

The overarching goal across all research activities is to produce evidence that is methodologically sound, contextually grounded, and directly applicable to health system improvement. Research is designed to identify gaps in service delivery, understand behavioral and structural drivers of health outcomes, and support evidence-informed decision-making at community, facility, and system levels. Emphasis is placed on practical relevance, ethical rigour, and clear translation of findings into action.

Research combines quantitative and qualitative approaches to capture both measurable outcomes and contextual understanding. Quantitative methods, including structured surveys and secondary data analysis, establish patterns and statistical associations. Qualitative components, such as interviews and focus group discussions, provide depth and explanatory power that numbers alone cannot offer.

All research is grounded in ethical responsibility: protecting participant dignity, ensuring informed consent, and maintaining transparency throughout analysis and reporting. Community-based research emphasizes participatory principles wherever feasible. Findings are returned to participating communities in accessible formats as a matter of ethical practice, not academic courtesy.

Research outputs are designed with translation in mind from the earliest stages, packaged for academic, policy, and practitioner audiences to maximize utilization. This approach aligns with the adaptive programming principles promoted by OECD DAC evaluation standards and the USAID Learning Lab's emphasis on evidence-to-action cycles.

Published research is made available through open-access repositories including Zenodo, ensuring findings reach practitioners and policymakers who lack institutional journal access, particularly across low- and middle-income country contexts where the research itself is situated.
