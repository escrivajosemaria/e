Health systems and programmes

Designing health systems that work in practice

My work sits between the programme plan and the people expected to deliver it: coordinating community health activities, monitoring what is actually happening in the field, and using data to find where delivery is slowing down.

The challenge

Moving beyond vertical interventions

Effective health programmes need to work within the wider system they sit inside, not as isolated interventions. A vaccination drive succeeds or fails based on cold chain logistics, health worker availability, and community trust, not on the vaccine alone. Programmes that treat one problem on its own rarely produce lasting change.

My work sits at the intersection of programme design and frontline delivery: mapping the real constraints that keep good intentions from becoming good outcomes.
Framework

The building blocks of delivery

Six interconnected levers define how health systems perform at scale. In practice, most of the work happens at the seams between them: a supply chain failure shows up as a workforce problem, and a governance gap often shows up first as a data problem.

a

Service delivery

Safety, quality, and continuity of care across every level of the health system, from the community to the facility.

b

Health workforce

Staffing patterns, training, and supervision that determine whether frontline plans are actually carried out.

c

Supply chain

Inventory and logistics systems that keep vaccines, medicines, and supplies where they are needed, when they are needed.

d

Health information

Moving from paper registers to routine data that programme teams can actually use for decisions.

e

Governance

Local leadership, transparency, and accountability structures that hold a programme together once the initial funding attention fades.

f

Community

Community health workers and local actors, integrated into the formal system rather than treated as an add-on to it.

Systems thinking

Mapping the intersection

Sustainable impact requires understanding how supply chains, policy environments, workforce dynamics, and human behaviour interact, and where small changes produce outsized results.

I bring a structured, practical lens to health programmes: identifying the feedback loops and bottlenecks that a conventional assessment, looking at one component at a time, tends to miss.

Portfolio

Four programmes that show what this work has actually involved: the problem on the ground, my role in it, how the work got done, and what changed as a result.

St. Martha HospitalProgramme Associate

Coordinating six community health programmes and closing the reporting gap

Context

St. Martha Hospital ran six community health programmes covering preventive care, vaccination, and maternal and child health. The programmes had outgrown the coordination and reporting systems holding them together.

My role

I coordinated activity across the six programmes, supported outreach and training for community teams, and managed the reporting flow between the community level and hospital leadership.

How I worked

I planned and ran outreach and training sessions with community health workers and clinical staff, strengthened referral pathways between the community and the facility, and set up a routine reporting cycle using DHIS2, Excel, and Power BI.

Evidence

Vaccination records, training attendance, referral logs, and community feedback were tracked together, so the team could see whether outreach was actually converting into uptake and trust.

Process

How I work

The same six-stage cycle runs through the programmes above, in different combinations depending on where I sat in each one.

01

Assess

Understand the programme context, examine available evidence, and work with the team to identify the operational bottlenecks behind a performance gap.

02

Co-design

Work with health workers, community actors, and programme teams to shape a practical response, one that people on the ground can actually carry out.

03

Implement

Turn the plan into coordinated activity: outreach, training, referrals, and the day-to-day workflows that keep a programme running.

04

Monitor

Track indicators, collect and verify field evidence, and gather feedback so implementation problems surface early rather than at evaluation.

05

Learn and adapt

Use what monitoring and community feedback actually show to adjust delivery, rather than sticking to a plan that the evidence no longer supports.

06

Scale

Document what worked, strengthen the underlying systems, and support the parts of an approach that are genuinely ready to be sustained or extended.
