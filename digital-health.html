How information moves

From community to decision

Seven stages carry a piece of health information from the point it is collected to the point it changes what a programme does next.

01
Community

Households and service users are where the questions start, and where the answers have to make sense.

02
Data collection

Structured surveys and forms captured in the field through KoboToolbox and ODK.

03
Health information

Records consolidated into DHIS2 and other programme reporting systems.

04
Analysis

Cleaning, cross-checking, and summarizing in Excel and Power BI before anyone treats it as fact.

05
Programme decision

Findings reviewed against targets and field context by the programme team.

06
Field action

Outreach, staffing, or messaging adjusted based on what the data actually showed.

07
Learning

Outcomes tracked and fed back into the next round of questions.

Where it starts

Data in the field

Ask→Collect→Check→Analyze→Interpret→Act

Digital health work rarely starts at a screen. It starts with a good question, a form a field team can actually complete, and a plan for checking what comes back before anyone treats it as fact. That discipline has produced more than 800 household and service-user surveys, over 500 client feedback responses collected each year, and support to more than 25 outreach activities, mostly gathered through KoboToolbox and ODK and reported through DHIS2, Excel, and Power BI. Tightening that process on one programme cut reporting delays by roughly 30 percent.

From field data to programme decisions

Digital health in practice

Case 01
Community health data

Context

Community-level programming needed structured information from households and service users, not just anecdotal reports.

Role

Supported digital data collection and organized the resulting programme information.

Tools

KoboToolbox, ODK, Excel.

Process

Collect, clean, review, summarize, then feed findings back into programme planning.

Evidence

Over 800 surveys, more than 500 feedback responses a year, support to 25+ outreach activities.

Case 02
Health programme reporting

Context

Reporting depended on manual steps that slowed down how quickly field activity reached a decision.

Role

Worked on the reporting flow connecting field data to the summaries used in MEAL reviews.

Tools

DHIS2, Excel, Power BI.

Process

Field activity, data capture, verification, analysis, reporting, decision.

Evidence

Reporting delays reduced by roughly 30 percent through improved MEAL and data practices.

Case 03
Immunization data & MEAL

Context

An immunization campaign in hard-to-reach communities needed accurate coverage figures and a way to track community response.

Role

Supported post-campaign coverage surveys, data verification, and monitoring and reporting.

Tools

Field coverage surveys, verification, MEAL reporting.

Process

Data verification alongside community response and campaign learning.

Evidence

More than 3,000 children reached, 98 percent coverage, and a 30 percent increase in vaccine acceptance and uptake.

Data quality

Before I trust the number

Every figure in a report has a history. These are the questions I ask before it goes any further.

  • 01Where did it come from?
  • 02Was it collected consistently?
  • 03Is anything missing?
  • 04Does the number make sense?
  • 05What does the field context say?
  • 06What decision will this support?

Person → community → health worker → programme → data → decision.

Technology only earns its place when it shortens the distance between the two ends of that line.
