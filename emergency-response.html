Red Cross in practice

Humanitarian work at community level

Kenya Red Cross Society Life Member | Community Health & Humanitarian Response

As a Life Member of the Kenya Red Cross Society, I have taken part in community humanitarian and health activities that put the Red Cross principles into everyday practice: showing up in communities, listening to people, and connecting them to services.

This is membership and volunteer involvement, not a staff position. The work is field-level: walking households, running health and safety sessions, identifying people who need referral, and working alongside volunteers and community leaders.

How response moves

One connected system

Each step depends on the one before it. A signal that is never assessed, or a referral that is never followed up, breaks the chain.

  1. 01

    Detect

    Signals from communities, volunteers, and facilities.

  2. 02

    Mobilize

    Volunteers, leaders, and teams brought together.

  3. 03

    Assess

    Needs and risks checked on the ground.

  4. 04

    Respond

    Services and messages delivered to households.

  5. 05

    Refer

    People linked to care and support.

  6. 06

    Monitor

    Activities and feedback tracked and reported.

  7. 07

    Recover

    Follow-up, and lessons carried forward.

Four ways the work shows up

These are representative response scenarios based on community-level humanitarian and health work. They illustrate how I approach and support response activities and are not records of specific deployments.

Response Scenario

Community Health & Humanitarian Outreach

Situation
A community needs health information, basic needs assessment, and a way to reach services.
Role
Outreach volunteer supporting household visits and community sessions.
Action
Household engagement, health education, needs identification, referrals, and a feedback channel.
Field Focus
Reaching the households least likely to access services on their own.
Outcome
Needs recorded, referrals made, and community concerns fed back to the team.
Response Scenario

Outbreak Response

Situation
An infectious-disease outbreak such as mpox or COVID-19 raises fear, rumours, and demand for information.
Role
Community mobilizer supporting risk communication and volunteer coordination.
Action
Prevention messaging, referral pathways to health facilities, field reporting, and rumour and feedback tracking.
Field Focus
Clear messages, early referral, and community trust.
Outcome
People know where to go for care, and the team hears community concerns early.

From response to recovery

The work continues after the emergency

Community-level response connects to follow-up, referral systems, community trust, monitoring, and programme improvement. Each of these decides whether the next response is faster and better than the last.

  1. Response

    Immediate needs met and people reached.

  2. Stabilization

    Services and routines restored, with community feedback guiding priorities.

  3. Referral

    People with ongoing needs linked to facilities and support.

  4. Recovery

    Follow-up visits and monitoring, keeping community trust intact.

  5. Learning

    Findings written into training, procedures, and the next preparedness plan.
